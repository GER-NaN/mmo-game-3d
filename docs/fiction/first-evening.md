# First evening

**Fiction, not design.** Written 2026-09-21 from `docs/world.md` as of that date, by
the model. Nothing here is canon. Each scene ends with what it drew on, by world.md
section and source tag, and with the gaps it hit: places where the world does not say
and the scene had to stop, hedge, or step around. The small inventions (a name, a
street, the colour of the sky) are listed at the end so they are never mistaken for
design.

---

## 1. Carried

She wakes with her cheek on a bench and someone's coat over her legs.

"There she is." A woman in a bus company fleece is crouched at eye level, and behind
her two men are already walking away, arguing about whose turn it was to carry. "You
went down hard out past the ridge. Don't go out there without water, love."

Tamsin sits up. The name is hers; she chose it on the island, and she chose the hair,
too, the blue that is now full of grass. The island is gone. She remembers the boss, the
size of it, the moment she understood that she was meant to lose, and then the fall.
Everything she carried on the island is gone with it: the GPU she built from drone
scrap, the defence drone she traded it for, the parts, the dollars. Her pockets are
empty except for one thing.

The phone is small, black, and warm, as if someone had held it the whole way here. She
does not remember being given it. The bag she opens is otherwise bare. The phone's
label says it plainly: equippable, a door to the terminal world. Battery ten percent.

The town is Old Town, and it is evening. The sky is the pale green that comes just
after sunset, and the street lamps along the square are lit, warm and low. Down the
side street to the east they are not. The dark starts at the corner like a wall.

Nobody in the square is looking at her. A man with a pram. Two teenagers on the fountain
rim, one of them laughing at something on his phone. A bus pulls in, and its display
reads a stop name she does not know. It is an ordinary evening in an ordinary town, and
she is the only person in it who knows why the east side is dark.

> **Drawn from:** the Training Grounds exit, faint, lose everything, wake in a town,
> the phone as the reward (section 16, Q12, Q14, Q16, Q23). Fainting carries you to the
> nearest town by helpful strangers (section 8, Q1). No player death (section 18). A name and a look
> chosen at the start, hair colour included (section 6, Q27). Old Town as the hand-built hub
> (section 10, T1). Real clock, evening lighting (section 14, C-2026-09-18). Starter phone in the
> inventory with a description saying it is equippable (section 4, C-2026-09-21). Ten percent
> on arrival (section 9, Q16). Street lights dark or lit, noticeable at night (section 12, Q20).
> Society functions, people blissfully ignorant of the fight (section 1, B, Q16).
>
> **Gaps:**
>
> - What HP she wakes with after the exit faint. The world says fainting has a cost,
>   but not what the first minute in town looks like on the meter.
> - Whether "helpful strangers" are NPCs, and whether they say anything, or whether
>   the carry is invisible and she simply appears. The scene made them people.
> - How the phone is "given". The world says it is the reward for leaving. The scene
>   could not show a giver, so it is just in the bag.
> - What a new player wears. The look is chosen on the island; the scene assumed the
>   clothes survive the faint even though everything else is lost.

## 2. Ten percent

She stands under a lamp, because it feels safer, and takes the phone out.

There is a way to do it. Hand to pocket, phone up to the face, head down. The square
does not go away. It fogs. The fountain, the teenagers, the bus are still there at the
edges of her eyes, going about their evening in grey, and she could not touch any of
them now if she wanted to. In the middle of everything is the phone.

The screen is cold. Cold in the way the island's terminal was cold, the same OS, just
less of it. A short column of apps. Chat. TODO. Log. Most of the rest wear a small lock
and a line under the name: not available on this device, or not available yet. She can
read every name. FPV drone surveillance, locked. Exchange rate, showing a number she has
no wallet to spend. Status board, and on it, scrolling, a line about a data centre raid
somewhere far to the west, in progress, no way to join.

The battery bar in the corner is red.

She opens Chat. It says Old Town Chat at the top, and under it a scatter of lines from
people who are, somewhere in the fog around her, also standing still with their heads
down.

> **KoolGuy78:** anyone doing the east grid tonight or am I
> **vex:** did it last week, it's back off, something keeps hitting the firewall
> **KoolGuy78:** LFG substation, need 1

She opens TODO. It is a list, and it is short. *Street light grid aaa-001: dark. Robo
taxi 14: rootkit, do not board.* One more line, greyed, that she cannot take yet. She
opens Log. *vex repaired street light grid aaa-001, four days ago.* Above it, older
lines, other names, the same grid, again and again.

She is reading the fourth one when the screen goes black.

Not off. Gone. The fog lifts off the square all at once and the evening is loud again,
the fountain, the bus pulling out, and the phone in her hand is a piece of warm glass.
Zero percent. She had been online for a few minutes.

> **Drawn from:** the phone's centred view with the world in peripheral fog, no
> movement, head-down pose (section 4, section 7, C-2026-09-21). Warm physical world, cold terminal
> (section 17, C-2026-09-15). The terminal OS as apps; locked apps show with a notice; the
> exchange rate before you have a wallet (section 4, Q7, Q33). Status board of events elsewhere
> (section 4, Q31). Chat reachable from any device, "Old Town Chat", "LFG: Substation repair"
> (section 13, B, Q26). The TODO list, the town log with names and grids, robo taxis with
> rootkits (section 12, Q20). The AI randomly breaking firewalls and killing grid, a repair
> cycle (section 12, Q21). Three hours of use from full, so a few minutes from ten (section 7,
> C-2026-09-21). Zero while online: the phone shuts off and the connection is lost (section 4,
> C-2026-09-21).
>
> **Gaps:**
>
> - What losing the terminal connection costs. The world says "consequences, not
>   covered yet". The scene lets her lose nothing but the page she was reading, which
>   is a choice the world has not made.
> - What the phone layout actually shows. "A few objectives, GPS, chat" is all the
>   world says. The scene put Chat, TODO and Log on it and left the rest locked. GPS
>   is not shown because the scene did not know what it looks like or does in town.
> - Whether a player can see other players' chat while they are not online. The scene
>   assumed no: chat lives behind the door.
> - What "not available on this device" versus "not available yet" means, and whether
>   both notices exist. The world says locked apps show with a notice, not which kinds.
> - Whether a repair job can be "taken" by one player, and what the greyed line means.
>   The scene invented that a job can be unavailable to her.
> - The status board's raid line implies a data centre exists somewhere on day one.
>   The world says one region at first; whether any data centre exists in it is open.

## 3. The picnic table

A battery is not hard to want and, it turns out, not hard to find, and she does not
like that.

She walks the lit side of the square. There is a shop with its shutter half down and a
window full of phones and cables and a sign about a sale that ended. Closed. She has no
dollars anyway. Past it, along the railings of the park, there is a green metal bin, and
next to the bin, on the low wall, a battery. Sitting there. The right size. She stands
and looks at it for a while before she picks it up, because someone has left it, and
because nobody else has taken it, and neither of those makes sense to her.

It is at a hundred.

The park has a picnic table under a lamp, the ordinary kind, bolted down, initials
carved in the top, and it is also, she is fairly sure, the thing she needs. On the
island there was a bench with a vice on it. Here it is a table where two women are
finishing their coffee.

"You want it? We're done." They gather up their cups. One of them, going, says to the
other, "It was on the news again. Sewer plant in some town up north, they're saying the
AI did it." And the other says, "They always say that," and they are gone.

Tamsin sits. The table is a workbench the moment she treats it as one. Phone in the one
place. Dead battery out; it goes into her bag, and she has no idea what she will ever
do with it. Full battery in. It is the least dramatic thing she has done all day, and
when it is done the phone is warm again, and the bar is green.

> **Drawn from:** a new player's first purchase is a battery, found on the ground or
> bought in a shop (section 9, Q16, Q17; section 7, C-2026-09-21). A new battery is at a hundred;
> a dead one stays in the inventory or goes to a recycler (section 7, C-2026-09-21). The
> workbench: a picnic table in the park, item slot, component slot, apply; one or two
> per town in public (section 7, C-2026-09-21). The Training Grounds teaches crafting and
> trading (section 16, Q12). People hear "AI did this" on the news and treat it as normal;
> the sewer plant as a target (section 1, section 3, Q11, Q16). NPCs oblivious but not ignorant
> (section 1, Q16).
>
> **Gaps:**
>
> - **Where a battery comes from on day one, and why it is there.** This is the
>   scene's biggest hole and it knew it. "Found on the ground" is canon; a full battery
>   on a park wall that nobody took is not a world, it is a test mechanic, and world.md
>   section 18 says today's ground pickups are exactly that. The first playable needs a reason
>   a battery is where a new player looks: a giver, a bin, a job, a shop that takes
>   nothing.
> - What a new player has to spend. She lost everything on the island, so the shop is
>   closed to her by fiction and by fact. Whether the town gives a new arrival anything
>   (a few dollars, a first job that pays) is not in the world.
> - How a player knows a picnic table is a workbench. The scene gave her a hunch. The
>   world says they should not look the same, which makes the question sharper.
> - Whether two players can use one workbench at once, and whether NPCs "occupy" it.
>   The scene had the women leave first.
> - What the dead battery is for. The recycler is named and not built; the scene gave
>   her a useless object and no plan.

## 4. Grid aaa-001

Online again, under the lamp. The bar is green and the fog is back and this time she
goes straight to TODO.

*Street light grid aaa-001: dark.* She takes it. The line changes, the way a thing
changes when it has your name on it, and a marker she did not have before is on the
map, three streets east, in the dark.

She puts the phone away. The square comes back at full volume. And she walks toward the
corner where the light stops, past the last lamp, into a street she can see only by what
the moon does to the fronts of the houses. It is not far. It is far enough that she
understands why vex did it in daylight and why the log is full of the same grid, over
and over.

The junction box is on the wall beside a shuttered launderette, a grey cabinet at hip
height with a door that is not locked. Inside it is what she trained for on the island,
and also nothing like it, because on the island the wires were laid out for her and the
lights were a lesson. Here somebody, or something, has been at this box, and it was not
a person.

She takes the phone out again, and this time she does not put it away.

> **Drawn from:** the TODO list as the go-to for a first-time player; one repair job
> end to end; the lights come on for everyone (section 12, Q20, Q31; `first-playable.md` items
> 5 and 6). Repairs happen in the world (replace wires or terminals at the substation,
> repair lines) and on the terminal side (restore the firewall) (section 3, Q11). The AI hits
> the grid from the terminal side (section 3, Q11). Moonlight and night that reads
> (`first-playable.md`, art). The Training Grounds teaches the grid repair (section 16, Q20).
> Nothing hurts you in the first town (`first-playable.md`, not in it).
>
> **Gaps:**
>
> - **What "the work" is.** The scene stops at the open box with the phone out because
>   the world stops there. `first-playable.md` calls this the one design choice in the
>   list: a task in the terminal, a walk to the junction box, or both. The scene chose
>   "both" by walking her there and putting her online, and then could not write the
>   next sentence.
> - What "taking" a job does. The scene invented that a job gets your name and a map
>   marker. The world has a TODO list and a log and nothing between them.
> - Whether the map and GPS work on the phone in town, and what a marker looks like.
>   The island teaches the map and GPS; the town scene had to assume they exist.
> - What the AI did to the box, physically. The world says the AI attacks the grid from
>   the terminal side, and players repair lines and replace wires. A box hit from the
>   terminal side with nothing physically wrong would make the walk pointless; a box
>   physically damaged needs a story for how. The scene said "not a person" and left
>   it.
> - Why the same grid fails again and again. The world allows a random repair cycle
>   (section 12, Q21). Whether a player can see why, or stop it, is the difference between a
>   chore and a fight.
> - Whether a street with no lamps is dangerous in the first town. The world says
>   nothing hurts you there. The scene played the dark as dread anyway, because dread
>   is free. If the town is ever AI-controlled, this street is where the Tesla is.

## 5. Everyone

It is not a big thing when it happens. That is the point.

The lamp above the launderette comes on first, then the next, then the row, each one a
half-second after the last, the way a string of lights does when the fault is fixed at
one end. The street goes from moon to warm. A cat leaves. Across the road a window that
was dark shows a kitchen, and a man in it, who looks up at the lamp outside as if it
had said his name, and then goes back to the sink.

She stands in it for a moment. Then she takes the phone out, because she wants to see
it written down.

*Tamsin repaired street light grid aaa-001.* Just now. The line above it is vex's, four
days old. Hers is on top.

> **KoolGuy78:** east side's on
> **KoolGuy78:** who
> **vex:** new name. hi Tamsin
> **vex:** it'll be off again by friday. welcome to old town

She does not answer yet. She is looking at the row of lamps down to the corner, and at
the square beyond them where nobody looked up, and at the bar in the corner of the
screen, which is a little less green than it was.

> **Drawn from:** when a repair is done the lights come on and everyone knows, because
> the lights appear (section 12, Q20). The town log with the player's name (section 12, Q20). Chat
> from any device (section 13, Q26). A small win brings local reputation (section 12, B). NPCs
> oblivious to the fight (section 1, Q16). The random repair cycle (section 12, Q21). Battery drains
> in use (section 7, C-2026-09-21). Player-made change is permanent and visible; the world
> remembers what you did (section 1, Q0).
>
> **Gaps:**
>
> - What the repair pays. The world says an objective's reward screen lets the player
>   choose a currency or item, and that a small win brings an immediate upgrade and
>   local reputation. The scene paid her nothing but the lights and a line, because the
>   world has not said what a street grid is worth.
> - Whether reputation is visible at this scale. "Player zooer66 is in town, everyone
>   gets +10% hacking" is canon; whether a first repair moves anything, and whether
>   others can see it, is not.
> - Whether a player's name in the log is their display name, which is not unique. Two
>   Tamsins would share the credit.
> - Whether other players get any notice when the lights come on beyond seeing them.
>   Push notifications reach the in-game phone for attacks; nothing is said for
>   repairs.

## What the story could not do

Summary of the gaps, for the backlog or a feature-design session, most load-bearing
first:

1. **The work of a repair.** The scene ends where the design does. This is the first
   playable's spine and its open choice.
2. **Where a battery comes from on day one, and what a new arrival has to spend.**
   Canon allows "on the ground"; the scene shows why that reads as a test mechanic.
3. **What losing the terminal connection costs.** Deferred in the phone session, and
   the scene had to make it cost nothing.
4. **What taking a job means**: a name on it, a marker, exclusivity, or nothing.
5. **What a repair pays**, and whether a first win shows anywhere but the log.
6. **How the AI's terminal-side attack shows in the physical world**, at a box a
   player walks to.
7. The phone's app list; the map and GPS in town; who the helpful strangers are; what
   the dead battery is for; how a workbench is recognised; HP on waking.
